# Brotato Golem Guide: Build, Weapons, Stats and Strategy

> **Quick Answer:** Golem trades normal in-wave healing for unusually strong Max HP and Armor scaling. RUNBACK tested a Rock-start defensive melee direction on Danger 0: Rock was easy to establish early, but by the Wave 8 shop the run had become a mixed six-weapon loadout with only two Rocks, and the build started feeling noticeably harder after Wave 8. The tested run reached Wave 12, but it did not clear Danger 0.

## Golem Traits and How They Work

Golem starts with a large health advantage and gets stronger value from Max HP and Armor upgrades than a normal character.

The current tested character screen shows:

- +20 Max HP
- Max HP modifications increased by 33%
- Armor modifications increased by 33%
- +40% Attack Speed while below 50% HP
- +20% Speed while below 50% HP
- no healing during the wave

That combination gives Golem a clear identity: build enough durability to survive each wave, but do not mistake durability for infinite safety.

## Can Golem Heal?

During a wave, Golem cannot heal.

That means normal sustain tools such as HP Regeneration, Life Steal, and consumable healing should not be treated as core investments for this character.

The important distinction is that **no healing during the wave does not mean permanent damage across the whole run**. According to the source data used for this page, Golem's HP is restored between waves.

In practice, each wave gives you a limited HP budget. Damage taken during that wave matters because you cannot repair it until the wave ends.

This is why deliberately taking damage just to activate the below-50%-HP passive is not the baseline recommendation here.

## Why Rock Is a Simple Starting Weapon

Rock is an easy starting direction because Golem can begin with it, it belongs to the Primitive and Blunt classes, and its damage uses Melee Damage.

That makes the early plan easy to understand:

- start with Rock
- take useful Max HP and Armor
- build enough Melee Damage and general offense to keep clearing
- add reasonable weapons when the shop does not keep offering more Rocks

Rock is a starting direction, not a mandatory final loadout.

Higher-tier Rocks can also provide defensive stats, which fits Golem's general durability plan, but this test did not prove that forcing Rock all run is reliable.

The early game was straightforward.

By the Wave 3 shop, the test had naturally reached three Rocks without forcing a perfect shop.

- Max HP: 63
- Armor: 3
- Melee Damage: 1
- Attack Speed: 5%
- Speed: -4%

The useful takeaway is not "always get three Rocks."

It is that a Rock-first start can form naturally enough in the first few waves.

Do not overspend rerolling just to chase six Rocks. If the shop stops offering enough of them, keeping a functional loadout matters more than preserving a pure theme.

## Golem Stat Priorities

This is a direction, not a set of exact thresholds.

### Max HP and Armor

These are Golem's clearest defensive priorities because the character gets increased value from both.

The test visibly grew from 63 Max HP / 3 Armor at the Wave 3 shop to 85 Max HP / 7 Armor at the Wave 8 shop.

That does not make 85 HP or 7 Armor a target.

They are simply recorded points from this run.

### Melee Damage, Attack Speed and General Damage

Defense alone was not enough to keep the run comfortable.

By Wave 8, the build had:

- 16 Melee Damage
- 25% Attack Speed
- 4% general Damage

The test became harder after Wave 8, so Golem still needs enough offense to stop enemy pressure from snowballing.

Do not treat the low general Damage number as the proven single cause. It was only one pressure among several.

### Speed and Dodge

Speed should not be ignored.

The test had **-4% Speed** at both the Wave 3 and Wave 8 snapshots, and the player specifically identified Speed as one of the problems later in the run.

That does not establish a minimum Speed threshold.

It only shows that defensive stacking can become awkward if your movement gets too weak for the enemy density you are facing.

Dodge can also support survivability, but this test did not establish it as mandatory.

## Why the Test Became Harder After Wave 8

The run reached Wave 12, but it became noticeably less comfortable after Wave 8.

The player reported that continuing to progress required taking much more damage.

Several pressures were present at the same time:

- the loadout had become mixed
- general Damage was still limited
- Speed was negative
- Golem could not heal during the wave
- enemy pressure was increasing

The test suggests that survivability alone was not enough to keep later waves comfortable.

That does **not** mean Wave 8 is a fixed Golem difficulty spike.

It only describes what happened in this run.

## Should You Stay Below 50% HP?

No fixed rule.

The below-50%-HP passive activated naturally during the test, giving the character the listed Attack Speed and Speed bonuses.

But the player described the practical difference as only **slight** in this short test.

Because Golem cannot heal during the wave, intentionally taking damage to activate the passive is risky.

A safer interpretation is:

**treat the passive as a comeback bonus when your HP drops naturally, not as a reason to seek damage.**

## Alternative Golem Weapons

Rock is not the only possible direction.

### Spiky Shield

Spiky Shield is a natural alternative to consider because its damage scales from Armor.

That can fit Golem's defensive stat direction well.

However, Spiky Shield requires an unlock, so it should not be presented as available to every player by default.

### Revolver and Slingshot

These can be used as alternative weapon directions when the shop does not support the Rock plan cleanly.

They should be treated as alternatives, not ranked above or below Rock on the basis of this test.

The key point is adaptability.

If the shop gives you a functional weapon that fits the rest of your stats better than another expensive reroll, taking the usable option can be better than forcing a pure Rock setup.

## Common Mistakes

### Buying Healing as if Golem Can Use It Normally

Golem cannot heal during the wave, so do not build around HP Regeneration, Life Steal, or consumable healing as if they work normally in combat.

### Ignoring Offense Because Golem Looks Durable

Max HP and Armor are important, but later waves still require enough damage output to control enemy pressure.

### Forcing a Pure Rock Loadout

This test had three Rocks at Wave 3 but only two Rocks in a six-weapon loadout by Wave 8.

The shop may not support a pure Rock setup naturally.

### Ignoring Speed

The tested run had negative Speed and the player later identified movement as one of the pressures.

### Deliberately Taking Damage to Activate the Passive

The below-50%-HP bonus did trigger naturally, but the practical difference felt only slight in this test.

Because there is no in-wave healing, deliberately taking damage is not the recommended baseline.

### Assuming Rock Scales From Armor

It does not.

Rock uses Melee Damage.

### Assuming Spiky Shield Is Available by Default

It is not.

Spiky Shield requires an unlock.

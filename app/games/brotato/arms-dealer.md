# Brotato Arms Dealer Build: Shop Strategy, Economy and How to Play

> **Quick Answer:** Arms Dealer loses every weapon when entering a shop, so the character is less about preserving one perfect loadout and more about permanent stats, material economy, and rebuilding a usable weapon set every wave. RUNBACK's Danger 0 melee-leaning test reached Wave 12, but the run failed after material management became increasingly restrictive and rerolls plus purchases became harder to afford.

## Arms Dealer traits

Arms Dealer changes the normal Brotato weapon loop.

The character has **-95% Weapons Price**, starts with **+30 Harvesting** and **1 Dangerous Bunny**, gets **+33% to Damage modifications**, and is guaranteed at least one weapon offer in each shop.

The defining drawback is more important than any of those bonuses:

**All of your weapons are destroyed when entering a shop.**

Arms Dealer's current Wiki entry also lists **Pistol** as the character's starting weapon.

That means the weapons you use during one wave are temporary. Your lasting build is mainly your permanent stats, items, and economy.

For this RUNBACK test, Arms Dealer was played on Danger 0 with Pistol.

## How Arms Dealer actually works

The first shop makes the mechanic obvious.

RUNBACK started Wave 1 with a Pistol. After the wave ended and the first shop opened, the Pistol was gone and the weapon counter showed **0/6**. This directly confirmed the character's weapon-destruction mechanic in the tested run.

![Wave 1 shop showing 0/6 weapons](BRT-ARMS-02.png)

*The Wave 1 shop in the test. Weapons are at 0/6 because the starting Pistol was destroyed when the shop opened.*

The basic loop is:

1. Finish a wave with whatever usable weapons you bought for that wave.
2. Enter the shop.
3. Your current weapons are destroyed.
4. Rebuild a temporary weapon set.
5. Keep enough materials for future rerolls and purchases.
6. Fight the next wave.
7. Repeat.

This is why Arms Dealer should not be treated like a normal fixed-loadout character.

The important thing to build permanently is not one exact weapon set. It is a combination of **stats + economy** that can support different temporary weapon groups from wave to wave.

## The tested melee-leaning setup

This RUNBACK test used a melee-leaning flexible approach.

That does not mean every weapon had to be melee.

The permanent stat direction leaned toward **Melee Damage**, general **Damage**, and **Attack Speed**, while the shop was used to assemble whatever reasonable temporary weapon set was available.

When useful melee weapons appeared, they were preferred because permanent Melee Damage remained useful across changing melee weapons.

But the run did not force a perfect six-melee shop every wave.

A rigid recipe such as:

- 6 × Cacti Club
- 6 × Spear
- six copies of any other exact weapon

is not supported by this test.

The more useful principle is:

**Build permanent stats in a direction, then use the shop flexibly enough to survive the next wave without bankrupting the run.**

## The shop loop: buy, fight, lose weapons, rebuild

Weapons are extremely cheap for Arms Dealer, but that does not make the whole shop cheap.

The shop is entered after every wave. Rerolling costs materials, repeated rerolls within the same shop become more expensive, and locked entries can remain between shops.

Dangerous Bunny gives one free reroll per shop.

This creates the character's main economic tension:

**You need weapons now, but you also need materials later.**

You still need materials for two separate jobs:

- finding usable weapons through rerolls
- buying those weapons once they appear

Spending aggressively can make one wave safer while leaving the next shop harder to recover from.

Saving too aggressively can leave your current temporary weapon set too weak.

The goal is therefore not simply:

**fill six slots at any cost.**

It is:

**buy enough usable weapons for the next wave while keeping your economy alive.**

## Should you fill all six weapon slots?

Not necessarily.

During this Danger 0 test, roughly three usable weapons during some early waves felt workable enough that saving materials remained possible.

That is a player observation from one run. It is not a fixed threshold.

Do not read this as:

**Three weapons are always enough before Wave 6.**

Instead, use it as an economic principle.

Early on, a smaller but functional weapon set can sometimes be preferable to repeatedly rerolling just to force six ideal weapons.

When the economy allowed it, the test did build a full temporary loadout.

## How to use Lock and rerolls

Lock can help Arms Dealer carry a useful shop option forward.

In this test, RUNBACK:

1. locked a Cacti Club
2. entered the next shop
3. bought the locked Cacti Club first
4. rerolled
5. saw additional melee weapon options

That happened in this test, but it was only one sample.

It does **not** prove:

**Locking a melee weapon guarantees more melee weapons.**

The shop's weapon-selection logic can favor weapons you already own or weapons sharing a class with an equipped weapon.

So a practical sequence is:

**Lock useful weapon → buy it first next shop → reroll**

Buying the weapon first gives the reroll an equipped weapon and weapon class to work from.

But this remains probabilistic.

Use Lock to preserve a useful option and improve the next shop's starting position—not as a guarantee.

## Stat priorities

Arms Dealer benefits from permanent stats that remain useful after the temporary weapon loadout disappears.

For this melee-leaning test, the useful directions were:

### 1. Melee Damage

If you are leaning toward melee weapons when reasonable offers appear, permanent Melee Damage supports those changing weapons.

This test does not prove melee is Arms Dealer's best overall build.

It only validates a melee-leaning flexible direction through the tested portion of Danger 0.

### 2. General Damage

General Damage is especially useful for a flexible character because it can continue helping even when the next shop forces you into a different weapon mix.

By the Wave 8 shop, this test had reached **62% Damage**.

### 3. Attack Speed

Attack Speed is also permanent and can support many temporary weapons.

By the Wave 8 shop, the recorded setup had **57% Attack Speed**.

### 4. Harvesting and material economy

This is the most important lesson from the failed run.

Arms Dealer already begins with +30 Harvesting, but this test also found that economy needed deliberate attention.

By the Wave 8 shop, the recorded run had **54 Harvesting**.

Even so, the player's conclusion was that material management earlier in the run had not been strong enough.

A good Harvesting value later does not automatically mean earlier spending was efficient.

You still need enough materials to:

- reroll
- find usable weapons
- actually buy them

### 5. Survivability

Do not completely sacrifice survival stats for economy and damage.

## Why economy matters more than it looks

Arms Dealer's -95% Weapons Price makes weapons look almost free.

But the weapon itself is only part of the cost.

The real pressure comes from finding the weapons.

Repeated rerolls become more expensive during the same shop.

If you spend too much trying to force a perfect loadout, you can reach a point where:

1. you need more rerolls to find good weapons
2. rerolls consume your remaining materials
3. a useful weapon finally appears
4. you no longer have enough materials to buy it

That is the economic failure pattern this run began to encounter.

A useful way to think about it is:

**poor early material management  
→ less reroll flexibility  
→ fewer acceptable choices  
→ greater dependence on favorable shop rolls  
→ less money left to buy what appears**

This does not mean Arms Dealer is purely RNG.

A more accurate conclusion from this test is:

**Poor material management made the tested run increasingly dependent on favorable shop rolls.**

## Why this run failed at Wave 12

RUNBACK's Danger 0 test reached Wave 12 and failed during or around that wave.

The main lesson was economic.

The player did not conclude that Melee Damage itself had stopped working.

Instead, the run had become increasingly constrained by the cost of rebuilding weapons.

Later shops required enough materials to do both:

**reroll + purchase**

When that material buffer became too small, weak shop rolls became harder to recover from.

So the correct conclusion is not:

**Wave 12 is where Arms Dealer falls off.**

It is:

**In this test, the run became economically constrained and failed around Wave 12.**

For another attempt, the clearest adjustment would be to preserve more materials earlier, make good use of the free reroll, build economy when sensible, and avoid forcing six ideal weapons every shop.

## Arms Dealer FAQ

### Why does my weapon disappear?

Because Arms Dealer's weapons are destroyed when entering the shop.

That is the character's normal mechanic, not a bug.

### Should I buy six weapons every shop?

No fixed rule was proven.

During this Danger 0 test, roughly three usable weapons sometimes felt sufficient during early waves, while later shops sometimes supported a full 6/6 temporary loadout.

The objective is a usable set—not automatically six weapons at any cost.

### Should I play melee or ranged?

This test used a melee-leaning flexible direction.

Permanent Melee Damage was emphasized and reasonable melee offers were preferred, but the test did not force pure melee every wave.

It does not prove melee is better than ranged.

### What should I lock?

A weapon that fits your permanent stat direction and that you would genuinely want to buy at the start of the next shop is a reasonable candidate.

RUNBACK tested the idea with Cacti Club.

### Does locking a melee weapon guarantee more melee weapons?

No.

RUNBACK saw additional melee options after locking and then buying Cacti Club before rerolling, but that was one test.

The shop's weapon-selection system can be influenced by equipped weapons and their classes, but it does not guarantee the next result.

### Why do I run out of money later?

Because Arms Dealer pays not only for weapons but also for finding them.

Repeated rerolls cost more, while you still need enough materials remaining to buy the weapons you find.

If the early economy is spent too aggressively, later shops can leave you short on both rerolling power and buying power.

### Did RUNBACK clear Danger 0 with this setup?

No.

The test failed during or around Wave 12.

This run provides evidence for the early/mid-game shop loop, economy pressure, and a melee-leaning flexible strategy.

It is not evidence of a complete Danger 0 clear.
